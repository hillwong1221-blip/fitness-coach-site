深蹲為什麼會歪斜？
骨盆、腳掌、髖控制的完整分析

深蹲歪斜不是「腿不夠強」的問題。從臨床肌動學角度拆解三層失衡根源，並提供系統化改善策略。

Why Your Squat Shifts —
The Complete Guide to Pelvic, Foot, and Hip Control

Squat misalignment isn't simply "weak legs." A three-layer breakdown of the root causes — and a systematic correction strategy.

by Coach Hill

引子

你是否注意過，有些人做深蹲時身體會歪向一邊？

有人以為是「腿不夠強」，有人怪「核心不夠穩」。但真正的答案通常更複雜。

深蹲歪斜的三層問題

表面症狀：身體向左或向右傾斜
真正原因：腳掌、髖部、軀幹的協調失調

第 1 層

腳掌壓力分配

重心偏向外側或內側，骨盆被迫跟著歪斜。

第 2 層

髖部控制

臀中肌、臀大肌無法穩定骨盆，蹲下時骨盆「滑移」。

第 3 層

軀幹穩定性

核心不足導致脊椎側彎代償，重心線偏移。

第 1 層：腳掌壓力分配

你的腳底承載著整個身體的重量。如果重心偏向外側或內側，骨盆就會被迫跟著歪斜。

  • 外側足（腳掌外側受力過多）→ 骨盆偏離中線
  • 內側足（大拇趾失控）→ 髖關節外旋受限
  • 足弓塌陷 → 整條腿的力量傳導路線改變

第 2 層：髖部控制

髖關節是連接下肢與軀幹的樞紐。如果髖部肌肉無法有效穩定骨盆，骨盆就會在蹲下時「滑移」。

  • 臀中肌無力 → 對側骨盆下沉
  • 髖外旋控制不足 → 膝蓋內扣
  • 髖屈肌過緊 → 骨盆前傾或後傾過度

第 3 層：軀幹穩定性

核心肌群的工作不只是「挺直」，而是動態穩定脊椎。當核心力量不足時：

  • 脊椎會側彎代償 → 整個軀幹歪斜
  • 肋骨位置改變 → 呼吸效率下降
  • 重心線偏移 → 下肢承重不均

如何診斷你的問題在哪一層？

這需要系統化的檢測，而不是憑感覺。我們通常會：

  1. 靜態評估 — 站立時腳掌、膝蓋、骨盆、肩膀的對齊狀況
  2. 動態檢測 — 在蹲動過程中觀察骨盆的移動、腳掌的壓力點、軀幹的穩定性
  3. 功能測試 — 單腳蹲、跨步、側向蹲，找出哪個環節最先失控

改善策略（由下而上）

步驟 1：修正腳掌著地

  • 學習足弓激活 — 大拇趾和小拇趾同時用力
  • 練習三點支撐（腳跟、大拇趾、小拇趾外側）
  • 初期可用瑜伽磚或平衡墊加強本體感覺

步驟 2：強化髖部穩定

  • 側向步行（Sidestepping with band resistance）— 強化臀中肌
  • 單腳立姿轉身 — 練習髖關節動靜態控制
  • 髖外旋力量訓練 — 棒式、蛤殼式、側向蛤殼式

步驟 3：建立軀幹穩定

  • 抗側彎訓練（Pallof press、單臂農夫行走）
  • 動態棒式 — 在穩定的基礎上加入動作變化
  • 呼吸訓練 — 腹式呼吸 + 核心激活

步驟 4：整合動作

  • 淺蹲 → 平蹲 → 深蹲漸進
  • 持續監測骨盆偏移
  • 漸進增加負荷（啞鈴、槓鈴）
如果你只做深蹲，卻沒有先修正腳掌和髖部，你就是在強化一個錯誤的動作模式。這樣做只會讓問題越來越嚴重。

快速自檢清單

如果你勾選了 3 項以上，你的深蹲可能需要「重新校準」。

  • 做深蹲時，身體向左或向右傾斜？
  • 膝蓋內扣或外翻？
  • 蹲下時感覺重心不穩，想往前傾？
  • 蹲到底時，一邊的臀部感覺更緊張？
  • 做了很多深蹲但腿部力量沒進步？

深蹲歪斜影響你的訓練進度嗎？

透過動作評估找出你的失衡根源，讓每一次蹲都是真正的訓練。

預約動作評估與優化課程

Opening

Have you ever noticed someone's body shifts to one side during a squat?

Some blame "weak legs." Others say "poor core stability." But the real answer is usually more complex.

The Three Layers of Squat Misalignment

Surface Symptom: Body tilts left or right
Real Cause: Dysfunction in foot, hip, and torso coordination

Layer 1

Foot Pressure Distribution

Pressure shifts to outer or inner edge — pelvis is forced to follow.

Layer 2

Hip Control

Glute max and glute med can't stabilize the pelvis — it "slides" during descent.

Layer 3

Torso Stability

Insufficient core leads to lateral spinal flexion and uneven leg loading.

Layer 1: Foot Pressure Distribution

Your feet bear the entire body's weight. If pressure shifts toward the outer or inner edge, the pelvis is forced to follow.

  • Lateral foot dominance (excessive outer loading) → Pelvic shift
  • Weak arch / foot collapse → Altered force transmission up the leg
  • Big toe dysfunction → Hip external rotation limitation

Layer 2: Hip Control

The hip joint connects your lower body to your trunk. If hip muscles can't stabilize the pelvis effectively, it "slides" during the descent.

  • Weak glute medius → Contralateral pelvic drop
  • Poor hip external rotation control → Knee valgus
  • Tight hip flexors → Excessive pelvic tilt

Layer 3: Torso Stability

Core function isn't just about "standing tall" — it's about dynamic spinal stabilization. When core strength is insufficient:

  • The spine compensates with lateral flexion → Entire torso shifts
  • Rib position changes → Breathing efficiency drops
  • Center of mass shifts → Uneven loading on legs

How to Diagnose Which Layer Is Your Problem

  1. Static Assessment — Foot, knee, pelvis, and shoulder alignment while standing
  2. Dynamic Testing — Observe pelvic movement, foot pressure, and spinal stability during squat descent
  3. Functional Movements — Single-leg squat, lunges, lateral squat to identify which layer breaks down first

Correction Strategy (Bottom-Up)

Step 1: Restore Foot Mechanics

  • Foot arch activation — simultaneous big toe and pinky toe engagement
  • Three-point support practice — heel, big toe, lateral foot edge
  • Proprioceptive drills on unstable surfaces (yoga block, balance pad)

Step 2: Build Hip Stability

  • Sidestepping with band resistance — glute medius activation
  • Single-leg stance rotations — static and dynamic hip control
  • Hip external rotation strength — plank variations, clamshells

Step 3: Establish Torso Stability

  • Anti-rotation training (Pallof press, single-arm farmer carries)
  • Dynamic planks — stability with added movement
  • Breathing mechanics — 360° breathing + core engagement

Step 4: Integrate the Movement

  • Shallow → quarter → full depth progression
  • Monitor pelvic alignment continuously
  • Gradual load increase (dumbbells → barbell)
If you only perform squats without first correcting foot and hip mechanics, you're reinforcing a faulty pattern. This only compounds the problem over time.

Quick Self-Assessment Checklist

If you checked 3 or more, your squat likely needs a recalibration.

  • Body shifts left or right during squat?
  • Knees cave inward or flare outward?
  • Feel unstable at the bottom, tempted to lean forward?
  • One glute tighter or more engaged than the other?
  • Lots of squat volume but no leg strength gains?
