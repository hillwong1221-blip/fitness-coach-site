肩膀不舒服 ≠ 肩膀夾擠症
— 從排球選手的案例看肩膀功能恢復

肩膀不適不一定是夾擠症。透過 CAK 臨床肌動學系統評估，找出動作控制的真正問題。

Shoulder Discomfort ≠ Shoulder Impingement
— A Volleyball Player's Recovery Journey

Shoulder pain doesn't always mean impingement. A CAK clinical kinesiology assessment reveals the real movement control issue.

by Coach Hill

引子

Star 是一位資深排球選手與教練。

右肩無力、活動受限、做過頭動作時不適 — 這是她來診的主訴。但問題真的只是「肩膀夾擠」嗎？

為什麼只放鬆不夠？

很多人遇到肩膀不適，第一反應就是找治療師放鬆肌肉。但 Star 的情況不同：她不只需要放鬆，更需要重新建立肩膀的動作控制。

一位運動員每天都在做極端的過頭動作（扣殺、上手發球），肩膀處於長期的開放鏈、高負荷狀態。時間久了，大腦就會「忘記」正確的動作順序。

用 CAK 臨床肌動學檢測找出真正的問題

我們沒有急著放鬆，而是問了三個關鍵問題：

  1. 活動度與功能有沒有恢復？ — 肩胛、胸椎、頸椎是否能做出應有的動作？
  2. 是否有隨伴代償？ — 當肱骨移動時，肩膀有沒有用「壞習慣」代償？
  3. 為什麼會這樣？ — 是肌肉張力問題？還是動作控制丟失了？

檢測結果

CAK 評估發現

上斜方肌 + 胸肌張力異常偏高 ↑
旋轉肌群相對力量偏弱 ↓
肩胛骨下轉速度過快，限制肩外旋及外展 ↑
核心問題肱骨與肩胛骨連動控制障礙

這個情況非常接近「肩夾擠症候群」的表現，但根源不在夾擠本身，而在於動作鏈的失序。

訓練調整的邏輯

針對檢測結果，我們設計了以下四個階段：

第 1 階段：放鬆過度張力肌肉

  • 上斜方肌的動態伸展
  • 胸肌的動態放鬆
  • 建立肩關節的基本活動空間

第 2 階段：強化弱化肌群

  • 棘下肌、小圓肌的穩定性訓練
  • 旋轉肌群在各角度的等長訓練
  • 低負荷、高控制的外旋動作

第 3 階段：重建肩胛動作控制

  • 開放鏈位置恢復肱骨與肩胛的協調
  • 牆壁俯臥撐變化訓練
  • 肩胛骨上轉的主動控制練習

第 4 階段：漸進式負荷

  • 從基本動作回到開鏈高位動作
  • 逐步恢復扣殺、上手發球等專項動作
  • 建立自我維持的訓練習慣

結果

放鬆加訓練後，Star 的肩膀不適大幅改善，活動度恢復，能夠安心執行過頭動作。

最重要的是：她學會了如何自我維持肩膀的健康狀態。

你的肩膀問題也是夾擠嗎？

肩夾擠是一個籠統的診斷。如果你的肩膀不適反覆出現，放鬆也只是短期緩解，那麼問題可能不在肌肉本身，而在動作控制。

你的肩膀問題也反覆出現嗎？

透過動作評估，找出你的肩膀問題的根本原因，而不只是暫時緩解。

預約肩膀功能評估

免責聲明：以上內容為調整訓練建議，非醫療診斷或治療。本案例未使用任何治療性質的手法。

Opening

Star is an experienced volleyball player and coach.

Right shoulder weakness, limited mobility, and discomfort during overhead movements — that's what brought her in. But is the real problem simply "shoulder impingement"?

Why Stretching Alone Isn't Enough

When people experience shoulder discomfort, the first instinct is often to find a therapist for muscle release. Star's case was different: she didn't just need relaxation — she needed to rebuild shoulder movement control.

An athlete performing extreme overhead movements daily (spiking, serving) puts the shoulder in a prolonged open-chain, high-load state. Over time, the nervous system "forgets" the correct movement sequence.

Finding the Real Problem with CAK Clinical Kinesiology

We didn't rush to release muscles. Instead, we asked three critical questions:

  1. Has mobility and function been restored? — Can the scapula, thoracic spine, and cervical spine move as expected?
  2. Are there compensatory patterns? — When the humerus moves, is the shoulder relying on "bad habits"?
  3. What's the root cause? — Is it muscle tension? Or has movement control been lost?

Assessment Findings

CAK Assessment Results

Upper trapezius + pectoralis major toneHypertonicity ↑
Rotator cuff relative strengthWeakness ↓
Scapular descent speedToo fast — limits external rotation & abduction ↑
Core findingBreakdown in humeral-scapular coordinated control

This presentation closely resembled "shoulder impingement syndrome," but the root cause wasn't impingement itself — it was disruption in the kinetic chain.

The Training Adjustment Strategy

Based on the assessment, we implemented a four-phase approach:

Phase 1: Release Excessive Muscle Tension

  • Dynamic stretching for upper trapezius
  • Pectoralis major dynamic release
  • Restore basic range of motion at the shoulder

Phase 2: Strengthen Weakened Muscles

  • Infraspinatus and teres minor stability work
  • Rotator cuff isometric training at multiple angles
  • Low-load, high-control external rotation movements

Phase 3: Rebuild Scapular Movement Control

  • Restore humeral-scapular coordination in open-chain
  • Wall push-up variations
  • Active scapular upward rotation practice

Phase 4: Progressive Loading

  • Progress from basic movements to overhead open-chain
  • Gradual return to spiking and serving patterns
  • Build sustainable self-maintenance habits

Results

After release and training, Star's shoulder discomfort significantly improved, mobility was restored, and she could confidently perform overhead movements.

Most importantly: she learned how to maintain shoulder health independently.

Is Your Shoulder Problem Also Impingement?

Shoulder impingement is a broad diagnosis. If your shoulder discomfort recurs repeatedly and stretching provides only temporary relief, the issue may not be muscle tension — it could be faulty movement control.
